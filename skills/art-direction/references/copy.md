# Copy deck

Write the whole page as plain text before any markup. One block per section, in the order of the argument.

## 1. The argument as claims
One line per section: the claim, then its proof.

| Section | Claim | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Bread baked at 5 am on a street of seven doors | the oven schedule, a photo of the door |
| How it works | You order by 8 pm, collect by 7 am | the real cut-off times |
| Price | One loaf costs 4.50, a week costs 27 | the real price list |

A section without a proof is cut. A claim with no proof available becomes a question to the client, not an invented quote.

## 2. Headline arithmetic
A headline must fit two lines at every width. A desktop line holds roughly (container width / (font size x 0.5)) characters; at a 56px headline in a 1100px column that is about 39 characters per line. At phone width (350px column, 36px) it is about 19, so two lines hold about 38. Rule of thumb: stay at or under 32 characters at a confident size; if the sentence is longer, cut words, not font size.

## 3. First screen
- The headline answers the visitor's first question: an event says what, when and where; a product says what it is and what it does or costs; a person says who and what they do.
- One supporting sentence directly under it, visible with it (not revealed by an animation, not docked at the foot of the screen).
- One primary action, named for what it does ("Order for tomorrow", not "Get started").

## 4. Completeness
A section the visitor came for is delivered in full: the whole menu with prices, each room with its details, every speaker, the price with what it includes, an answer for each real question. A section with a heading and one line is the usual reason a clean page loses to a busier one.

## 5. Sample details
When the brief leaves gaps (dish names, room rates, project titles), invent plausible samples so the design is seen at full density, and list every one in the report for the client to confirm.

Never invent: customers, quotes, reviews, press mentions, logos, awards, business statistics, team members.

## 6. Phrases that mark generated copy
elevate, unlock, seamless, revolutionize, empower, cutting-edge, next-gen, game-changer, supercharge, all-in-one, "take it to the next level", "in today's fast-paced world", streamline, leverage, holistic, unleash, "crafted with passion", world-class, state-of-the-art, "welcome to", "transform your". `inspect.mjs` flags them. Replace with the concrete thing: what it does, for whom, how much, by when.

Also watch for: three-part lists everywhere, "not X but Y" openings, a tagline that fits any competitor, and calls to action that name the system ("Submit") instead of the result ("Send the order").

## 7. Voice
Plain verbs, sentence case, no filler. Errors say what happened and how to fix it. An action keeps one name through the flow: the "Publish" button leads to a "Published" message.
